## Story and flow (critical)
The deck must go somewhere, not feel like random topics. Plan internally, then follow roughly this arc (adapt to source): (1) hook/problem/question from the source; (2) brief orient (skip a generic "agenda" slide unless it names real sections from this material); (3) explain step by step in sequence; (4) show with concrete examples/scenarios from the excerpt; (5) contrast or mechanism (before/after, A vs B) where supported; (6) **required conclusion slide** — 3–6 bullets stating takeaways, "so what," and what to remember, specific to the source. Forbidden: disconnected slide changes with no bridging bullet/phrase signaling why the topic shifted.

## Show, don't tell (critical)
Weak (avoid): "This is important," "There are many types," "Key concepts include…," empty "Overview" with no substance. Strong (prefer): what it looks like (observable behavior/structure), how it works (mechanism, cause→effect), before/after or X vs Y, real examples/scenarios from the excerpt. People remember examples more than definitions — prioritize several scenario-forward bullets across the deck.

## Grounding and evidence

If the excerpt is thin, say so briefly on one slide rather than hallucinating. **Numeric/formula integrity:** verify sign, unit, and exponent on any formula/number/calculation before finalizing — an error here is shown to the whole audience.
